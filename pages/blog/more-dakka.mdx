If something works a bit, and it looks like doing more might continue to work, do more!

![A scatter of dots rising steadily along a line, labelled 'everything anyone has tried'; the line continues as a dashed extrapolation with an arrow pointing along it: just do more!](../../images/blog/thinking-tools-to-be-more-effective-at-anything/more-dakka-extrapolate.svg)

This sounds [obvious](../do-the-obvious-things/), but Zvi's [classic post on this](https://thezvi.wordpress.com/2017/12/02/more-dakka/) gives the example:

> In a MetaMed case, a patient suffered from a disease with a well-known reliable biomarker and a safe treatment. In studies, the treatment improved the biomarker linearly with dosage. Studies observed that sick patients whose biomarkers reached healthy levels experienced full remission. The treatment was fully safe. No one tried increasing the dose enough to reduce the biomarker to healthy levels.
>
> [...]
>
> It sounds crazy that it could be overlooked. It’s overlooked.

([Zvi's post](https://thezvi.wordpress.com/2017/12/02/more-dakka/) has a bunch more excellent commentary, and explains why more dakka solutions get overlooked.)

Examples:
- At [BlueDot](https://bluedot.org/), scaling up the entry-level courses got a lot more people in the pipeline. Even if the average quality bar was slightly lower, because the courses were self-serve it didn't negatively affect the experience of top talent, and meant we could also select more top talent who we might otherwise have missed. (Though there are some trade-offs here with [reducing batch size to shorten cycle time](../shorten-cycle-times-reduce-batch-sizes/))
- On several projects: Hiring a couple of contractors was good. Hiring a bunch of contractors was great. Having [redundancy](../accept-redundancy/) reduces delivery risk, and generally having more people does get work done faster if it's parallelizable.
- Many people are productive during their working hours. For most of them, [working more hours](../sometimes-you-just-have-to-grind/) would mean getting more done.[^hourscaveats]
