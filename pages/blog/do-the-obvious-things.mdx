When you have a problem, it's easy to dive into doing the first thing that comes into your mind to solve it.

![A flowchart with only two steps: '1. List the obvious things to do' then '2. Do them', ending at 'solved (usually)'](../../images/blog/thinking-tools-to-be-more-effective-at-anything/obvious-flowchart.svg)

But stopping to think through what "obvious" things a reasonable person would do with this task can help a lot. Checklists, formal decision-making frameworks like [PIOSEE](../piosee/), and asking other people (and AI assistants like Claude) for obvious advice can also help with this.

See Nate Soares' ["Obvious advice"](https://mindingourway.com/obvious-advice/) for more.
